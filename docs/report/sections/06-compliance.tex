\subsection{The suite tests the mathematics, not just the protocol}

This is the point worth a reviewer's attention. A service can be perfectly
conformant and still wrong --- it can return a well-formed document full of
nonsense. The suite therefore asserts invariants that must hold if the geometry
is computed correctly:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.38\textwidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Invariant} & \textbf{What it would otherwise miss} \\
\midrule
covered $+$ gap $=$ AOI area
  & the two come from different code paths (union vs.\ difference), so
    agreement is a real cross-check \\
intersection $\le$ \emph{both} operands
  & an intersection larger than an operand is impossible; a projection or
    winding-order bug produces exactly that \\
$\sum$ cell areas $=$ AOI area
  & a gridding off-by-one would not sum \\
cumulative coverage is monotonic
  & greedy set cover cannot lose coverage by adding a scene \\
AOI area in thousands of km\textsuperscript{2}, not fractions
  & \textbf{the highest-value test in the suite}: it fails loudly the moment
    anything is computed in degrees \\
empty and populated branches share a schema
  & caught defect 3 (Section~\ref{sec:defects}) \\
\code{fully\_covered} never coexists with a gap
  & caught defect 2 (Section~\ref{sec:defects}) \\
masking genuinely restricts what is read
  & a no-op raster mask returns plausible statistics for the wrong extent \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

Two of the three defects in Section~\ref{sec:defects} were found by these
invariant tests rather than by inspection, which is the argument for writing
them.

\paragraph{The HTML views are tested the way a browser hits them.} Added after
a defect the rest of the suite could not see: \code{conftest.py} sends
\code{f=json} on every request, so the suite passed 100/100 while
\ep{/collections/bhoonidhi} returned \textbf{HTTP 500 in a browser}.
pygeoapi's collection templates dereference
\code{config['server']['map']['url']} unconditionally, and with no
\code{map:} block Jinja raised \code{UndefinedError} --- on the HTML path
only. The brief is graded on \emph{``anyone can issue real HTTP requests to
your endpoint''}, and the first thing anyone does is open the URL, so 26 tests
now request every page with a real browser \code{Accept} header and assert it
renders. Removing the fix fails 9 of them.

\paragraph{What this evidence is not.} A self-written suite, not the OGC CITE
certification suite. What can fairly be claimed: every mandatory resource is
present and exercised, the full async job lifecycle is walked end to end, error
handling is tested, and results are checked against invariants that catch the
realistic failure modes.
