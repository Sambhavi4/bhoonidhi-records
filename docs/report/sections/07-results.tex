\section{Live demonstration}
\label{sec:results}

Base URL: \ep{http://localhost:5003}. Everything below is reproduced by
\code{./scripts/demo.sh}; full transcripts are in
Appendix~\ref{app:transcripts}. The AOI used throughout is
\code{[75.3, 13.2, 75.8, 13.9]} --- a district-sized window
(4\,191\,km\textsuperscript{2}) over Chikkamagaluru, Karnataka.

\subsection{The gap, closed: which scenes do I actually need?}

This is the demonstration that matters, because it is the operation
Section~\ref{sec:cost} measured the absence of.

\begin{lstlisting}
curl -X POST localhost:5003/processes/minimal-scene-set/execution \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"inputs":{"aoi":[75.3,13.2,75.8,13.9],"collection":"sentinel-2-l2a"}}'
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=json]
"candidates_considered": 30,
"scenes_selected":        2,
"scenes_avoided":        28,
"achieved_percent":   100.0,
"selection": [
  { "order": 1, "new_area_km2": 2517.58, "cumulative_coverage_fraction": 0.60067,
    "item_id": "S2A_MSIL2A_20240331T051651_R062_T43PER_20240331T113328" },
  { "order": 2, "new_area_km2": 1673.70, "cumulative_coverage_fraction": 1.0,
    "item_id": "S2A_MSIL2A_20240331T051651_R062_T43PEQ_20240331T113919" }
],
"algorithm": "greedy set cover; at each step the footprint adding the largest
              previously-uncovered area is taken. Greedy set cover is within a
              ln(n) factor of optimal and is not guaranteed minimal."
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{keyfinding}{Thirty scenes match the AOI. Two cover it.}
Ordering the other 28 is wasted download, wasted quota and wasted time --- and
today nothing in Bhoonidhi's API tells you that. The response also gives the
\emph{coverage curve}: the first scene buys 60.1\,\%, the second completes it.
A user who only needs 60\,\% coverage can stop after one.
\end{keyfinding}

\subsection{\code{aoi-coverage} --- coverage and explicit gap polygons}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=json]
"aoi_area_km2":      4191.2797,
"covered_area_km2":  4191.2797,
"coverage_fraction":        1.0,
"gap_area_km2":             0.0,
"scene_count":               30,
"fully_covered":           true,
"gap_threshold_km2":       0.01,
"gaps":                    null
\end{lstlisting}

The AOI is fully covered, so \code{gaps} is \code{null} rather than a hairline
sliver --- defect 2 of Section~\ref{sec:defects}. Where coverage is partial,
\code{gaps} carries the uncovered remainder as GeoJSON polygons, which is the
form a planner actually needs: not a percentage, but \emph{where} the holes
are.

\subsection{\code{multi-sensor-overlap} --- co-coverage for fusion}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=json]
"collection_a": "sentinel-2-l2a",   "scenes_a": 30,  "coverage_a_km2": 4191.28,
"collection_b": "sentinel-1-grd",   "scenes_b":  8,  "coverage_b_km2": 4191.28,
"overlap_area_km2": 4191.28,  "overlap_fraction_of_aoi": 1.0,  "co_covered": true
\end{lstlisting}

Optical $\cap$ radar in one call, instead of two searches and a manual clip.
A test asserts the overlap can never exceed either operand.

\subsection{\code{revisit-density} --- acquisition density grid}

\begin{lstlisting}
POST /processes/revisit-density/execution
  {"aoi":[75.3,13.2,75.8,13.9],"collection":"sentinel-2-l2a","cell_size_km":10}

grid 6x8 @ 10 km -> 48 cells - 30 acquisitions
per cell: min 15, mean 18.75, max 30, never-observed 0
\end{lstlisting}

Returned as a GeoJSON \code{FeatureCollection}, one feature per cell carrying
\code{acquisitions}, \code{cell\_area\_km2}, \code{row} and \code{col} --- ready
to render as a heatmap with no post-processing. Revisit is exactly twice as
dense in some cells as others; a flat list of scenes has no spatial
distribution in it at all.

\subsection{The raster tier}

\code{zonal-statistics} masks the raster by a polygon and aggregates the pixels
inside it:

\begin{lstlisting}
518,400 pixels over 478.9 km2 - elevation 613.7-1439.1 m, mean 746.4, sd 112.6
  613.7-778.8 m  ->  356.6 km2  (74.5%)
  778.8-943.8 m  ->   94.0 km2  (19.6%)
\end{lstlisting}

\code{scene-raster-profile} is the join between the two tiers. For every scene
footprint intersecting the AOI it clips the raster to that footprint and
reports what is underneath, ranked by overlap:

\begin{lstlisting}
S2A_..._T43PER   1538.0 km2   elevation 584-1874 m, mean 752
S2A_..._T43PEQ    801.3 km2   elevation 650-1890 m, mean 941
\end{lstlisting}

Two candidate scenes, genuinely different terrain underneath. Choosing between
them on \emph{what they actually contain} is impossible from a catalogue that
answers only \emph{does this intersect your box}.

\subsection{Asynchronous execution and the job API}

\begin{lstlisting}
$ curl -i -X POST localhost:5003/processes/aoi-coverage/execution \
    -H 'Content-Type: application/json' -H 'Prefer: respond-async' \
    -d '{"inputs":{"aoi":[75.3,13.2,75.8,13.9],"collection":"sentinel-2-l2a"}}'

HTTP/1.1 201 CREATED
Location: http://localhost:5003/jobs/834a6ffa-c659-11f1-8558-28c5d24dc1d2
Preference-Applied: respond-async
{"jobID":"834a6ffa-...","type":"process","status":"accepted"}

$ curl localhost:5003/jobs/834a6ffa-.../?f=json
{"processID":"aoi-coverage","status":"successful","progress":100,
 "created":"2026-10-12T16:25:31.100225Z","finished":"2026-10-12T16:25:31.113922Z"}

$ curl localhost:5003/jobs/834a6ffa-.../results?f=json      # HTTP 200
\end{lstlisting}

The conformant shape, in full: \textbf{201 Created}, a \code{Location} header,
\code{Preference-Applied}, a status document with timestamps, and results on
completion. A compliance test walks this whole sequence rather than checking
only that a 201 came back.

\subsection{Performance}

Measured end-to-end over HTTP on the reference deployment, synchronous
execution, same AOI as above:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Process} & \textbf{Response time} \\
\midrule
\code{minimal-scene-set}     & 0.009\,s \\
\code{aoi-coverage}          & 0.010\,s \\
\code{multi-sensor-overlap}  & 0.010\,s \\
\code{revisit-density}       & 0.018\,s \\
\code{zonal-statistics}      & 0.074\,s \\
\code{scene-raster-profile}  & 0.585\,s \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The four footprint processes answer in roughly 10\,ms: greedy set cover over
30 candidate footprints is not a demanding computation, which is rather the
point --- the work Bhoonidhi leaves to every user is work a server can do
in ten milliseconds. The raster processes are bounded by windowed reads
rather than by geometry, and \code{scene-raster-profile} is the slowest
because it clips the raster once per intersecting scene footprint.

The asynchronous path therefore exists for conformance, and for the larger
areas of interest a production deployment would face, rather than because the
demonstrated workloads need it.
